We will prove by mathematical induction on \( n \geq 1 \) that the ratio \(\frac{a(n+1) + 1}{a(n) + 1} = 2\). For the base case \( n = 1 \), we note that \( a(1) = S(1) = 1 \) and \( a(2) = S(2) - S(1) = 3 \), where \( S(2) = 2 \times S(1) + 1 + 1 = 4 \). Evaluating the ratio yields \(\frac{a(2) + 1}{a(1) + 1} = \frac{3 + 1}{1 + 1} = 2\), confirming the base case. For the inductive step, assume the statement holds for some \( m \geq 1 \). Using the recursive definition \( S(m+3) = 2 \times S(m+2) + (m + 3) \) and expressing \( a(k) = S(k) - S(k-1) \) for \( k \geq 2 \), we rewrite the ratio as \[
\frac{a(m+3) + 1}{a(m+2) + 1} = \frac{S(m+3) - S(m+2) + 1}{S(m+2) - S(m+1) + 1}.
\] 
Substituting the recursion gives the numerator as \( S(m+2) + m + 4 \) and recognizing that the denominator simplifies to \( S(m+1) + m + 3 \) by the recursive properties of \( S \). Hence, the ratio becomes \[
\frac{S(m+2) + m + 4}{S(m+1) + m + 3} = \frac{2 \times (S(m+1) + m + 3)}{S(m+1) + m + 3} = 2,
\]
which completes the inductive step. Therefore, by induction, the ratio \(\frac{a(n+1) + 1}{a(n) + 1}\) equals 2 for all natural numbers \( n \geq 1 \), proving the proposition.
